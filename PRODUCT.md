# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, hiring managers, fellow AI engineers and researchers, and people Vineeth meets at SF hackathons. Most arrive from a shared link (LinkedIn, a conference chat, a blog post) and skim on a laptop; a meaningful share open it on a phone. They want a fast answer to "who is this person, what has he actually built, and is he worth talking to," and the curious ones stay for the writing.

## Product Purpose

vineethsai.me is Vineeth Sai's personal site: portfolio, research record, and increasingly a home for long-form writing (the Room Sentinel teardown). It exists to show two things at once: a rigorous AI/ML engineer (AT&T, Nabu Tutor, Google, published research projects) and a curiosity-driven person whose passions (astrophysics, philosophy, football, F1, cricket, tennis, basketball, books) all orbit one question: how do things work. Success: a visitor leaves with a clear picture of the person, not just the resume, and the serious ones reach out or read the blog.

## Brand Personality

Curious, honest, alive. Cosmic wonder held together by engineering discipline. The voice is first person, simple, short sentences, no em dashes (Vineeth's own stated rule in the blog source). He is comfortable being earnest: the books and sports essays are sincere, specific, and personal. The site should feel like him: a dark observatory with a red pulse, not a corporate landing page.

## Anti-references

- Generic template portfolios (ThemeForest dev-portfolio look, identical hover-card grids with no story).
- Keyword-stuffed resume sites where skill lists do the talking instead of work and writing.
- Overclaiming or fake polish. His own ethos from Room Sentinel: the model is only allowed to phrase facts the camera actually saw. The site must not claim what the person cannot back.
- Corporate SaaS gloss (gradient meshes, glassmorphism everywhere, buzzword copy).

## Design Principles

Derived from his own writing, so the site practices what the person preaches:

1. **The writing is the star.** The deep-dive essays and the blog are the most "him" thing on the site. Surfaces should hand the mic to them, not bury them behind hover states.
2. **Honest by construction.** No stat, title, or claim the site cannot back. Stale facts (old job titles, dead forms) are bugs, not cosmetics.
3. **Cosmic but engineered.** Starfield wonder with black/white/red discipline. Restraint over spectacle; one signature moment (the 3D universe) instead of ten competing effects.
4. **Every state earns its beep.** From the buzzer lesson: silence reads as broken. Every interaction (form send, card tap, nav) gives clear feedback, including failure states.
5. **One heavy thing at a time.** Focus per screen. Do not make two sections compete for the same attention.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA targets on a dark theme: body text >= 4.5:1 against near-black surfaces. Full keyboard access to everything hover reveals (project links, dive cards). prefers-reduced-motion alternatives for all animation (already partially in place; must stay). Touch devices get first-class treatment: no information locked behind hover.
